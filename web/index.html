Par vs. Market

A visual explanation of how an old low-rate mortgage can become economically comparable to a smaller new mortgage at current rates — and why that matters for refinancing.

Deliberately simplified: fixed-rate, fully amortizing mortgages; identical 30-year remaining term; no prepayment, servicing, guarantee fee or credit-risk adjustments.

The surprising part: in this particular calibration the two raw monthly payment streams are nearly identical. That is not a coincidence. The old mortgage's market price falls until buying its $382/month stream produces roughly the return investors can get from a new 4% mortgage.
Scene 2

How does an investor “see” those future dollars?

The promised payments do not change. What changes is how much each future dollar is worth in today's dollars.

Future mortgage dollars

Required return / discounting lens
Investor's mental model

The dollars stay the same. Their value today changes.

If comparable mortgage assets yield only 2.25%, paying about $100,000 for this stream is reasonable.

When comparable assets yield about 4%, an investor will only pay roughly $80,000 for the same old stream.

Prototype economics are intentionally stylized. In the 30-year no-prepayment example, $100,000 at 2.25% pays about $382.25/month. Discounting that stream at 4% gives a present value of about $80,066; an $80,000 30-year mortgage at 4% pays about $381.93/month.
